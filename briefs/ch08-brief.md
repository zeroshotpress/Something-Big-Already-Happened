# Chapter 8: "What Now?"

**Subtitle:** The Evidence Is In -- The Hard Part Is What Comes Next

**Word count target:** 3,000 words

---

## Opening Hook

This book began with a viral essay and a market crash. It traced the predictions of the people who built these systems, measured the evidence against those predictions, and documented the human consequences. The scorecard is now clear: of 23 major predictions tracked, 2 are fully confirmed, 1 is realized, 5 are partially realized, and 7 are in progress. The most confident predictions -- the ones backed by exponential data -- were also the most accurate. The recursive loop is operational. The benchmarks continue to climb. The layoffs continue to mount. So we end where every honest analysis of this moment must end: with the admission that knowing what happened is easier than knowing what to do about it.

---

## Key Arguments and Supporting Data

### 1. Scorecard final tally: What the data shows
- 23 predictions tracked across 8 sources
- Status distribution:
  - Confirmed: 2 (METR doubling, SWE-bench progression)
  - Realized: 1 (GPT-5.3-Codex self-improvement)
  - Partially realized: 5 (Amodei's recursive AI, 90% AI code at Anthropic, Hassabis AGI evidence, Shumer's societal impact, Altman's AGI during Trump)
  - In progress: 7 (Altman AGI, Amodei job displacement, Amodei Nobel research, Altman novel insights, Hassabis AGI by 2030, Amodei country of geniuses, AI data center investment)
  - On track: 2 (EU AI Act, NVIDIA chip demand)
  - Early signs: 3 (Huang robotics, Shumer computer safety, Shumer medical research)
  - Too early: 3 (Amodei SW engineer replacement, Altman human surpass, Hassabis superintelligence)
- Average realization across all 23 predictions: ~37%
- Key insight: predictions with quantitative backing (METR, SWE-bench) were most accurate; qualitative predictions (AGI, superintelligence) remain hardest to assess

### 2. What remains unfulfilled -- and the timelines
- AGI (various definitions): In progress but definition-dependent; no consensus achieved
- Superintelligence: Too early; Hassabis said "at best a few years out"
- Nobel-level scientific research: 15% realization; AlphaFold succeeded but independent discovery remains distant
- 50% entry-level job displacement: 20% realization; trend is clear but full scope is 1-5 years out
- AI surpassing humans in all dimensions by 2030: 15% realization; excels at coding and reasoning but limited in physical-world and common-sense tasks
- Country of geniuses in a datacenter: 20% realization; ~2028-2029 timeline
- Remaining unfulfilled predictions cluster around 2027-2030, meaning the next 1-4 years are the critical period

### 3. The policy landscape: Two visions, neither complete
- **EU approach**: Comprehensive regulation (AI Act timeline)
  - Aug 2024: AI Act enters force
  - Feb 2025: Prohibited practices enforced
  - Aug 2025: GPAI governance obligations
  - Aug 2026: Full applicability
  - Aug 2027: High-risk system compliance
  - Strengths: Worker protection, transparency, risk classification
  - Weaknesses: May slow EU competitiveness; enforcement across borders is difficult
- **US approach**: Deregulation and infrastructure
  - Jan 2025: Trump revokes Biden AI Executive Order
  - Jan 2025: Stargate $500B commitment
  - Feb 2026: State-level laws creating patchwork (California AI law dispute)
  - Strengths: Speed, investment, competitiveness
  - Weaknesses: No worker protection framework; no transition plan; state-level patchwork
- Neither approach addresses the core challenge: what happens to the displaced workers?

### 4. The three scenarios
- **Scenario A: Managed Transition** -- Governments invest heavily in retraining, implement UBI or transition support, regulate deployment pace. AI becomes a productivity multiplier that raises living standards broadly. Requires unprecedented policy coordination.
- **Scenario B: Market-Led Disruption** -- Companies adopt AI at market speed, workers displaced faster than retraining can absorb, inequality widens dramatically before stabilizing. Historically the most common pattern for technological transitions.
- **Scenario C: Recursive Acceleration** -- The self-improvement loop accelerates beyond governance capacity. Capability gains outpace regulation, safety testing, and social adaptation. The scenario Amodei's "Adolescence" essay warned about.
- Current trajectory: somewhere between B and C, with elements of A in the EU

### 5. What the reader can do: Framework for action
- **For individuals**: Understand which tasks AI can and cannot do; invest in skills that complement AI (judgment, creativity, physical work, interpersonal); stay informed about the pace of change
- **For organizations**: Audit AI exposure honestly; invest in worker transition before layoffs become necessary; participate in policy development
- **For policymakers**: Study the EU model but adapt to local context; fund retraining at scale; require transparency in AI-driven workforce decisions; invest in safety research
- **For the AI community**: Embrace the responsibility that comes with building the most powerful technology in human history; publish safety research; support governance frameworks even when they slow development
- The book's thesis restated: The predictions came true. The evidence is overwhelming. The recursive loop is running. The only remaining question is whether we will shape this transition or be shaped by it.

## Closing

The book ends not with a prediction but with a question. Every chapter has shown that the people with the most information -- the CEOs, the researchers, the benchmark authors -- were right about what was coming. The data confirmed their warnings, often ahead of schedule. The recursive loop they feared is now operational. The question is no longer whether AI will transform the world. It is whether we will be ready. The evidence says we are not. The opportunity says we still could be. The title of this book is not a warning. It is a fact. Something big already happened. What we do next will determine whether that fact becomes a tragedy or a transformation.
