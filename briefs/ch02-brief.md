# Chapter 2: "The Chorus of Warnings"

**Subtitle:** How the People Building AI Tried to Tell Us What Was Coming

**Word count target:** 2,500 words

---

## Opening Hook

In January 2024, the World Economic Forum in Davos buzzed with cautious optimism about AI -- leaders debated regulation versus innovation, and the consensus was that transformative AI was years away. Exactly two years later, at Davos 2026, the tone was unrecognizable. Dario Amodei stood on stage and predicted that most software engineering work would be replaced within six to twelve months. Demis Hassabis maintained a 50% probability of transformative AGI by 2030 -- a timeline that no longer sounded ambitious but conservative. Between those two Davos gatherings, every major prediction about AI had either come true or accelerated.

---

## Key Arguments and Supporting Data

### 1. Dario Amodei: The most specific and alarming predictions
- May 2025: Warned AI could eliminate 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs in 1-5 years, potentially spiking unemployment to 10-20%
- January 2026: Published "The Adolescence of Technology," a 20,000-word essay warning AI will "test who we are as a species"
- January 2026: Predicted AI is 1-2 years from autonomously building the next generation (80% confidence, 50% realization)
- January 2026: At Davos, predicted software engineers replaced in 6-12 months
- January 2026: Envisioned a "country of geniuses in a datacenter" by ~2028-2029
- Scorecard status: His prediction about recursive AI improvement is already partially realized (GPT-5.3-Codex)

### 2. Sam Altman: AGI on a political timeline
- November 2024: "AGI will probably get developed during Trump's term (2025-2029)" -- rated 70% confidence, 40% realization
- June 2025: Predicted 2026 would see AI systems generating novel insights -- rated 65% confidence, 25% realization
- 2025: "AI surpasses humans in all dimensions by 2030" -- rated 40% confidence, 15% realization
- Pattern: Altman's predictions were less specific but anchored to political and commercial timelines

### 3. Demis Hassabis: The measured voice with urgent conclusions
- January 2025: Predicted "meaningful evidence of AGI in play in 2025" -- rated 55% confidence, 40% realization
- March 2025: Gave transformative AGI a 50% probability by 2030 -- rated 60% confidence, 30% realization
- 2025: "Superintelligence is at best a few years out" -- rated 40% confidence, 10% realization
- Hassabis was notably more cautious than Amodei at Davos 2026, but his timelines were still historically radical

### 4. Jensen Huang: Following the money
- 2025: Predicted AI chip demand would drive $500B in sales over 2025-2026 -- rated 85% confidence, 50% realization
- NVIDIA forecast $65B quarterly revenue for Q1 2026
- Predicted reasoning advances would unlock leaps in robotics and self-driving -- rated 50% confidence, 15% realization
- Huang's predictions tracked the infrastructure buildout: Stargate ($400B+), Microsoft ($80B), Meta ($65B), Amazon ($75B), Google ($100B)

### 5. The convergence: From divergent voices to a unified warning
- By late 2025, the previously divergent predictions had converged on a narrow window
- All major lab CEOs agreed: transformative AI capabilities were months, not years, away
- The investment data confirmed the conviction: $500B+ committed to AI infrastructure
- Microsoft Research identified 5 million white-collar jobs facing extinction
- EU AI Act progressed from framework to enforcement, signaling regulatory recognition of urgency

## Closing Transition to Chapter 3

These were not idle speculations from futurists on the conference circuit. They were predictions made by the people with the most direct knowledge of what was being built inside the labs. And the most striking thing about the chorus of warnings was not that they were loud -- it was that they were testable. The next four chapters examine the evidence. We start with the benchmark that became the scoreboard of the AI revolution: SWE-bench.
