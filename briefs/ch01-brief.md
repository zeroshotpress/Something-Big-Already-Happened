# Chapter 1: "Something Big Is Happening"

**Subtitle:** The Essay That Broke the Internet -- and Confirmed What Everyone Already Felt

---

## Opening Hook

On February 9, 2026, a 5,000-word essay by Matt Shumer -- the 26-year-old CEO of OthersideAI -- appeared online and was viewed more than 20 million times within days. It was not a research paper or a corporate announcement. It was a warning, written with the bluntness of someone who had stopped caring about diplomatic framing: "Nothing done on a computer is safe in the medium term." The timing was not coincidental. Three days earlier, Anthropic had released Claude Opus 4.6 and OpenAI had released GPT-5.3-Codex -- a model that helped build itself. In the 48 hours between those releases and Shumer's essay, more than a trillion dollars evaporated from global software stocks.

---

## Key Arguments and Supporting Data

### 1. The essay crystallized a latent crisis
- Shumer's essay was viewed 20M+ times, a magnitude usually reserved for celebrity scandals or natural disasters
- It followed the Feb 5-6 market crash triggered by Claude Opus 4.6 and GPT-5.3-Codex releases
- Software stocks had been sliding since Feb 4, 2026, extending global losses amid fears AI would disrupt SaaS business models
- The essay gave language to a feeling that had been building since late 2025

### 2. Core claims mapped to verifiable data
- **"Nothing done on a computer is safe"**: SWE-bench Verified scores rose from 2.7% (Jan 2024) to 79.2% (Feb 2026); METR autonomous time horizons reached ~5 hours
- **"AI is bigger than Covid in societal impact"**: Trillion-dollar market shifts; 276,000+ tech layoffs; policy upheaval across US and EU
- **"A century of medical research compressed into a decade"**: AlphaFold and AI drug discovery advances, though clinical trial bottlenecks remain
- **"AI will replace most software engineering work"**: Prediction scorecard rates this at 60% confidence, 30% realization as of Feb 2026

### 3. The prediction scorecard framework
- Introduce the book's analytical framework: each major prediction is tracked with source, date, predicted timeline, current status, evidence, confidence percentage, and realization percentage
- 23 predictions tracked from 8 sources (Altman, Amodei, Hassabis, Huang, Shumer, METR, EU, OpenAI, Microsoft Research)
- Status distribution: Confirmed (2), Realized (1), Partially realized (5), In progress (7), On track (2), Early signs (3), Too early (3)
- Key insight: the predictions rated highest confidence (METR doubling at 90%, SWE-bench progression at 85%) were also the most fully realized

### 4. Why this moment was different
- Previous AI hype cycles (2017 deep learning, 2022 ChatGPT) were followed by "AI winter" skepticism
- Feb 2026 was different because: (a) multiple independent metrics showed exponential curves, (b) the financial markets reacted with real fear, (c) CEO warnings became specific and near-term, (d) a self-improving model was released
- The gap between public perception and documented reality had never been wider

### 5. The book's thesis stated
- This book is not about what might happen. It is about what already happened -- and what the evidence says comes next
- The predictions are no longer speculative. They are being measured, tracked, and in many cases confirmed
- The question is no longer "if" but "how fast" and "what do we do"
